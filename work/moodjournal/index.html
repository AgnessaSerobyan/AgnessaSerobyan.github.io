Case 02 / Wellness · Mobile App · Emotional Tracking

MoodJournal✦

A mood journal,
from check-in to diary.

MoodJournal started as my own product idea. I developed it with a client over about three months, working on research, the app concept, UX/UI, branding, and a landing page.

01 / Context & design challenge

Making room for
a daily entry.

A mood tracker can record a feeling without leaving much room to explain it. A blank diary page can ask too much. I wanted to bring short mood check-ins and longer entries into the same app.

What I looked into

The research covered diary habits and how people express emotions. Interviews and diary research explored why people stop writing, what they want to keep private, and how much effort a daily entry feels like. Privacy, motivation, and the difference between writing for yourself and sharing with others were recurring topics.

Target audience

People who want to keep a personal diary or track their mood without writing a long entry every day.

03 / Interface & visual system

Familiar controls,
personal entries.

I used illustrated expressions for mood choices, blue for the main actions, and rounded cards throughout. Entries, the calendar, and statistics share the same controls, so the app stays familiar as people move between them.
