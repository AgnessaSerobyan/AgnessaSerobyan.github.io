Case 01 / AI language learning

LinguaStar✦

Language learning,
from setup to practice.

I was LinguaStar’s sole UX/UI designer and built the design from scratch. I worked on the learning flows, AI chat, feedback screens, and visual system. The selected product designs shown here shipped.

The design task

One app,
several ways to practise.

The app combines conversation, exercises, and review. I needed to connect those parts so people could see where to start, how to answer, and where to get help.

I designed the steps between screens as well as the screens themselves: choosing a pace, starting a reply, getting a hint, and reviewing a result. Text, voice, and word-choice exercises needed to fit within the same chat.

01 / Getting started

Choosing a level
and a practice pace.

I put the target level, timeline, and daily practice estimate together during setup. The next screen leads with a recommended lesson, with a separate option for people who want to explore on their own.

Setting expectations

The timeline sits beside the daily estimate so people can compare the goal with the time it asks of them.

Keeping a choice

The recommended route has the main button. Independent exploration stays visible as a secondary choice.

02 / Conversation & help

A way to start.
Help when it’s needed.

Reply suggestions offer a starting point below the tutor’s message. I kept translation and playback with the message they belong to, and placed hints, examples, and task controls above the reply field.

Exercise detail / building an answer

An answer
in progress.

I designed the unfinished answer, too. The sentence stays visible as it is built. Used word tiles are disabled, and the remaining choices sit below it.

The reply field, voice input, and help controls stay in the same places when the exercise changes.

03 / Results & review

Reviewing a session.
Choosing what’s next.

I gave session results, lesson review, and daily tasks their own layouts. Results separate comprehension from pronunciation, with Replay and Continue at the bottom. The task list uses checkmarks and progress bars to show what is finished and what is left.

Two kinds of feedback

Comprehension has a short summary. Pronunciation has its own rows, so the two kinds of feedback can be read separately.

After the result

The result leads to a choice: replay or continue. In the task list, finished activities look different from the ones still available.

More exercise and progress screens +

These screens show feedback for correct and incorrect replies, a completed gap exercise, and the progress overview.

04 / Visual system

The same patterns,
throughout the app.

I brought the type scale, colours, illustrated icons, and control states into one design board. Alongside them, I included examples from tasks, feedback, and progress screens to show how the system is used.

The basics. I set the type sizes, colour roles, spacing, and corner radii for the interface.

In use. Messages, lesson cards, navigation, and feedback reuse the same controls and state styles.

English and Arabic promotional layouts +

I used the tutor illustration and cosmic palette in both English and Arabic layouts, adjusting the composition for each reading direction. I have not confirmed where each promotional version was published.

What I delivered

What I designed
and what shipped.

My work covered the learning flows, mobile UI, interaction states, and visual system. The selected product designs shipped.

I don’t have post-launch learning or business metrics for this case.

The screens show separate design states, with example names, scores, prices, and progress. The phone mockups and design board were added separately to present the work.
